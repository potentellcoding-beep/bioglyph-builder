    Teacher guide, learning targets & accessibility

    Standard: Ohio 7.PS.1 — classify elements using physical properties. No family names, valence electrons or reactivity memorization required.

    • Model first: Ask “Is it a metal?”; discuss why evidence can eliminate cards.
    • Partner practice: Students explain one elimination aloud using “I removed ___ because ___.”
    • Language support: Questions are short. Check “Read questions aloud” to enable speech synthesis in supported browsers. You can read an answer by clicking the “Hear answer” button after a question.
    • Independent practice: Switch to the 16-element board; try to solve in fewer questions.
    • Privacy: No sign-in, server calls, tracking, or local storage are used by this file.

    Atomic-number questions use the middle remaining card to split the choices. Atomic numbers are identifiers, not physical properties. Carbon questions refer to graphite; graphite conducts electricity and can have a shiny surface. Boron and silicon are semiconductors, rather than good electrical conductors. The visible board groups elements by type for learning support. For harder gameplay, the question history is not a substitute for careful reading of the cards and results.

    Illustrated element cards are schematic. Shiny-surface questions use freshly exposed samples (crystalline boron and silicon, and graphite for carbon). Physical-state questions refer to approximately 20–25°C and 1 atmosphere; electrical conductivity refers to elemental materials under ordinary conditions.
